# Music export

The game's music is written in [Strudel](https://strudel.cc) and kept in
`assets/source/music/`. It plays on the main menu, and in game when enabled.
The game embeds the exported Ogg Vorbis blocks and `manifest.json` from
`assets/music/`.

| Source | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `library.strudel.js` | Instruments, helpers, every layer a block can use, the main harmony |
| `main.strudel.js` | The main track: drift, ascent, departure, cruise, solo, arrival, fanfare, return |
| `space.strudel.js` | The space pieces and their ramps |
| `funk.strudel.js` | The funk pieces (the main theme as space funk) and their ramps |
| `phonk.strudel.js` | The phonk pieces and their ramps |
| `links.strudel.js` | Transitions between the families (manifest only; never re-renders audio) |
| `music.strudel.js` | Generated by the exporter from the others; paste it into strudel.cc |

The music is made of blocks, eight bars long unless a block sets `bars`:
steady drift, depart, cruise and arrive with variations; 10 BPM ramp blocks
from 60 to 120 and back; the 120 BPM Orbital Fanfare; a 16-bar guitar solo;
and three families, space, funk and phonk, each with pieces at 60, 80, 100 and
120 BPM and 20 BPM ramps between them. Each block declares weighted possible successors
whose starting tempo matches its ending tempo, and the game chooses randomly at
each boundary. Strudel.cc uses the fixed demo order and a single live tempo;
the exporter renders the block-specific tempo for each bar.

**Instruments.** Every pitched voice is a Strudel General MIDI instrument in its
MIT-licensed FluidR3_GM version (the `instruments` table in the source; see
`assets/source/music/soundfonts/`). Electric piano chords and a second electric
piano's root accents, synth strings, synth bass, a square-lead arpeggio, a
chiff-lead sequence and a sawtooth lead carry the main texture; drawbar organ,
French horn and flute replies enter during departure, build through cruise and
the tempo ramps, and peak at arrival, with a vibraphone countermelody,
synth-brass stabs and a clipped string-section ostinato in the fastest blocks.
Celesta bells, music-box stars and an atmosphere pad colour the slow blocks and
the interlude. A bowed viola sings slow guide tones through drift, depart, the
alternate cruise, the second half of the solo and the wind-down.

**Synths.** Strudel's own synthesizers give the music its space-opera and
electronic character. A Jarre-style supersaw pad swells once a bar through a
slow phaser and a resonant filter sweep in the slow, space and departing
blocks, with gaps rather than a held drone. From the rise to 100 BPM through
arrival, French-house pumping supersaw chords swell in after each kick under a
slow filter sweep, and a progressive-house sawtooth pluck, opened by a short
filter envelope and echoing on the dotted eighth, builds from the rise to
80 BPM. Both fade out on the descent. Each instrument has a `boost` that brings it to the level the
earlier synth voice had, so the block levels keep their balance. Drums,
shaker, hats, ride and the interlude's radio-noise ticks remain Strudel
synthesizers and noise; tuned toms use the melodic-tom sample.

**Guitar.** A clean electric guitar enters in the rise to 70 BPM and plays short
answer phrases through the rises, depart, cruise and arrival, fading out in the
fall to 70 BPM. The rises to 90 and 110 BPM, the alternate cruise and arrival
blocks and the descent into the solo answer with two-note strums. A quiet
rhythm guitar enters in the rise to 90 BPM, stays through cruise and arrival
and fades on the descent. Chords in guitar parts are strummed: each string
starts slightly after the one before. The solo is a single 16-bar block: a
singing single-note line with six bends up into held notes, strummed chords
and double-stops at the phrase peaks, rests between phrases, and three short
note-by-note runs at 1.8 times the eighth-note rate on the clean guitar with
long sustain and a light reverb and echo, slurred legato: each run
is picked once and its other notes are hammer-ons and pull-offs (`h` in the
source's guitar notation), softer and with a gentler attack. A quiet arpeggio, the
rhythm guitar, organ and horns support it, and it has less echo and room effect.

**Funk pieces.** The old main theme, reworked as space funk with a space-jazz
colour. They play a D-dorian vamp with extended chords, two bars per chord
(Dm11, G13, Dm11, then B♭maj9 and A7sus, ending on A like every block), and
have two themes like the space pieces. Theme C, the main theme's melody, keeps
its note values at 60 and 120 BPM: `funk_60` sings it on a talkbox-style vowel
lead over slap bass, Rhodes, vibraphone arpeggios, a muted trumpet answering
in the gaps, a swung jazz ride and the laser harp; `funk_120` plays it at double
time on the talkbox and clavinet in unison over the full band, congas and
tambourine. Theme D, a syncopated clavinet riff that uses the B natural of
D dorian, plays at 80 and 100 BPM with slap bass, chicken scratch, horn-section
stabs, the trumpet and vibraphone; `funk_100` adds organ, congas and
tambourine. To fill out the band without clutter, horn-section lines (in
octaves) answer the melody in its gaps, a held "ooh" choir sits in the middle,
a Moog-style filtered sawtooth doubles the slap bass an octave down, and a
`glue` setting sends the clav, guitar, horns, talkbox and keys further into
the shared reverb with a short slapback echo on clav and guitar. These grow
towards 120 BPM, where quiet open hats join too.

**Phonk pieces.** Dark Memphis and drift phonk in space, over a minor cycle of
two bars per chord (Dm, B♭, Gm, A7, whose C sharp pulls back to D minor).
Theme E, a busy cowbell melody (two square waves a fifth apart), keeps its note
values at 60 and 120 BPM: slow and eerie over sliding 808s, a dark pad, a
lowpassed choir and vinyl crackle at 60, and drift phonk over the full kit at
120. Theme F, sparser, plays on a dark, distorted supersaw lead at 80 and 100
BPM; at 100 the cowbell doubles it an octave up. A picked bass guitar plays a
syncopated line above the 808s, which slide up into each bar's last notes, and
horn-section stabs punctuate each bar. The drums sit well under the bass: a
phonk kit (a heavy kick and a half-time snare),
trap hats with triplet rolls, a half-time clap, rimshots, toms, shaker and
open hats, growing with the tempo.

Each family has six 20 BPM ramps between its pieces, and `links.strudel.js`
connects every piece to the main track and the other families at its tempo.

**Space pieces.** Mostly inspired by space-opera albums, they hold each chord
for two bars (Dm9, B♭maj7, Gm9, A7sus) and send their space layers to a second,
seven-second reverb. They have two themes. Theme A, the
hook, is a constellation of wide leaps (fifths and ninths, one two-bar phrase
per chord, rising a little with each chord up to B♭6) played staccato on a
glassy FM bell-synth in the deep reverb; it keeps its note values at 60 and
120 BPM, so 120 is exactly double time. Theme B, at 80 and 100 BPM, is a French-house riff:
clipped, syncopated sixteenth notes high on each chord (up to A6) through a
resonant filter sweep, over a disco octave bass. `space` (60 BPM) has a halo
pad, glassy crystal notes, atmosphere notes over faint radio static, computer
beeps and boops (short, steady square and triangle tones from D minor
pentatonic in telemetry-like bursts), a quiet nod to the hook with a faint
echo, and a laser harp: a bright sawtooth pluck snapped open by a fast
resonant filter, sweeping up and down each chord in eighth notes through a
phaser and long echoes (double time at 120 BPM, like the hook). `space_80`
plays the riff over a soft rhythm box (light kicks, woodblock-like clicks and
maracas), with a quiet orchestral-harp glissando up the chord at each chord
change, as at 100 BPM. `space_100` adds the main drums, disco open
hats, chopped house chords (a high voicing on every offbeat under a slow filter
sweep) and a synth choir. `space_120` returns to the hook at double time with a
square an octave below, an echo canon (the hook an eighth late, as filtered
plucks filling its rests), the laser harp, the house groove, pumping chords,
an arpeggio and laser zaps. They are reached from the blocks
at their tempo (depart, wind and the rise to 80; cruise, the solo and the fall
to 100; arrival, the fanfare and the rise to 120) and return to them. Six
20 BPM space ramps (`space_rise_80` to `space_fall_60`) also link neighbouring
space pieces directly, easing every level from where one piece ends to where
the next begins (crossfading the themes), with orchestral-harp glissandi at
every chord change, so the music can stay in space while it speeds up or
slows.

Every bar uses four evenly spaced kick hits. Quiet ride and toms join the
arrival blocks and fade in and out with the tempo ramps; the upward ramps add
an even shaker pulse and electric-piano root accents that build into arrival.

To hear the source in a browser, open [strudel.cc](https://strudel.cc), paste
`assets/source/music/music.strudel.js` into its editor and click **Play**
(or press Ctrl+Enter). Use **Stop** (Ctrl+.) to end playback. The last `arrange`
line plays every part in a fixed order at the demo tempo. The game instead
uses the rendered OGG blocks with their tempo ramps and weighted transitions.
To audition one part in Strudel, replace `window.demo` in that last line with
a one-item list such as `['arrive_a']` and press **Update**. In the game,
select that part in the main-menu Songbook, as a list or (on desktop, with the
Map toggle) on a map that shows every part by section and tempo with arrows for
its possible transitions and their chances; it crossfades in over two seconds
and then follows its weighted transitions.

## Re-export

Requires Node.js, Google Chrome and ffmpeg. From the repository root:

```sh
npm ci --prefix tools/music
node tools/music/render.mjs            # only blocks whose sources changed
node tools/music/render.mjs --force    # every block
```

Set `CHROME=/path/to/chrome` if Chrome is not at the macOS default location.
Sampled instruments load from local copies in `assets/source/music/soundfonts/`
(see its README for sources, licences and how to add one), so exports need no
network access.

The exporter joins `library.strudel.js` and the family files into
`music.strudel.js` and evaluates them together. Each block records a hash of
what its audio depends on (the library, its family's file, the instrument
files and the export settings) in `assets/music/render-cache.json`, and a block
is re-rendered only when that hash changes: editing `space.strudel.js`
re-renders the space blocks, while editing the library re-renders everything.
Blocks removed from the sources lose their audio files. Transitions between
families live in `links.strudel.js`, which no hash includes, so changing them
only rewrites the manifest.

It renders offline in headless Chrome one bar at a time. Each bar's notes can
ring for nine seconds after its end. It adds bars at their exact start frames
and gives each block its own fixed gain: a block at the reference raw loudness
(-27 dB RMS) plays at -26.1 dB, others keep a fifth of their difference from it
(capped at 5 dB), and no block peaks above -6.1 dBFS, which leaves headroom
where one block's tail overlaps the next. So blocks keep a little dynamic
contrast, extra instruments add density rather than loudness, and a block's
level never depends on the others. Each Ogg includes its tail; the manifest's
`frames` is the exact join point, excluding the tail. The game starts the next
block there and plays the old tail beneath it. Vorbis preserves the sample count
without MP3 padding. The manifest also records each block's active RMS. When the
next block is louder, the sequencer eases its gain up over the first bar; a
quieter block enters at its exported level while the previous tail fades.

`node tools/music/render.mjs source.strudel.js output.ogg [--bars N]` renders a
single source instead: blocks to a folder as above (without the cache), or a
loop (`window.tempoMap`, or N bars at its `setcps` tempo) to one file, with what
rings past its end folded back onto its start.

Each bar gets a fresh page because Strudel keeps audio nodes between offline
renders. The exporter waits for its reverb impulse response before rendering.
`DEBUG_BARS=1` prints per-bar peaks and Strudel console messages. A silent bar
also produces a warning.
